The Second Chance Ranch on the Hill

A once-forgotten property gets a full second life as a family cabin retreat.

By Cabin Life Editors  |  Photos courtesy of the homeowners

Share:ftp✉
The renovated ranch now sits comfortably above the valley it once overlooked in disrepair.

[Placeholder body copy — final article text to come from Cabin Life editorial.]

When the family first walked the property, the old ranch house had been empty for the better part of a decade. The roofline sagged, the porch had given way in two places, and the pasture beyond it had gone to seed. It was, by any reasonable measure, a hard sell. It was also exactly what they were looking for.

Over the following eighteen months, the team stripped the structure back to its frame, rebuilt the foundation, and reworked the layout around the one thing everyone agreed was worth saving: the view from the hill. What emerged is less a restoration than a second chance — a cabin that keeps the bones of the original ranch while functioning, room for room, as the gathering place the family had always pictured.

"We weren't trying to make it new. We were trying to make it last another hundred years."

The kitchen anchors the main living space, opening onto a covered porch built wide enough for the whole extended family at once. Reclaimed beams from the original barn were milled down and reused throughout, a detail the owners insisted on even when it would have been faster — and cheaper — to start from scratch.

Reclaimed materials from the original structure were worked into the rebuild wherever possible.

What started as a fixer-upper gamble has become the family's most-used address on the calendar — proof, the owners say, that the best cabins aren't the ones you build from nothing, but the ones you're willing to save.
